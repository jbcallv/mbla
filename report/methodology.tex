\section{What is evaluated}\label{sec:interface}
At each delegation, \BC{}'s policy engine asks an untrusted helper to select, from the delegator's
$(C_i, P_i)$ with $P = (N, \plain, D)$, the authority the task needs. \BC{}'s checker decides what is allowed;
the helper only decides how much of the least-privilege benefit a delegation gets. The checks in our library
are advisory and let the benchmark simulate \BC{}'s checker offline. Table~\ref{tab:interface} fixes the
interface every helper is evaluated under; it follows the current draft and \BC{}'s adapter (\texttt{cmd/bc-mbla}).

\begin{table}[H]\centering\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{L{0.15\linewidth}Y}
\toprule
Task context & Task text written by the delegator; receiver identity and kind (local or remote); current bounds
$(C_i,P_i)$, presented as the candidate list; upstream context; optional signed manifest, shown as a hint only. \\
Candidates & One per grant in $C_i$, plus a narrower grant for each resource the context names under a granted
prefix; one per destination in $N_i$ without a parser (selected into $\plain'$); for a local receiver only, one per
volume mount $(v,\mu)$ in $D_i$. Not scored: destinations with a parser (S3, httpapi) follow $C$; peers are chosen
by the agent; the model endpoint is always kept. \\
Output & Candidate indices only, under a fixed JSON schema, in three tiers: required (the proposal), plausible
(the admission set $A$), unneeded. \\
Failure & Timeout, error, unparsable output, an out-of-range index or an unknown key refuses the delegation. No
fallback proposal. \\
Receiver & $C$ is bounded by the parent only. A proposal whose $P'$ is not within $\ceil$ is rejected whole;
the delegator never sees $\ceil$. \\
Recovery & Started by the tunnel. One atom or one destination per request, granted only if within the parent and
in $A$ (a destination without a parser must also be in $\plain_i$); the receiver installs a destination only within
$\ceil$. Volume denials are final. The cap $k$ counts requests, granted or not. \\
Deployment & \BC{}'s adapter may call the helper once with the root task and once with the task text, in parallel,
and keep the lower score. This applies in the end-to-end runs; standalone results are per call, as the helper
takes one task text per call. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\caption{Helper interface. Every number in the paper is measured under these rules.}\label{tab:interface}
\end{table}

The evaluation does not need to show that inference is safe: G1 bounds every output, and a fully compromised
helper reproduces at most the parent's authority. It shows that (1) inference removes authority that G1 alone
would pass downstream without breaking tasks; (2) bounding recovery by $A$ keeps an injected instruction from
turning a denial into a grant; (3) the benefit survives an adversary who targets the helper. Cost is the fourth
axis, because the helper is on the path of every delegation.

\section{Research questions}\label{sec:rq}
\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1 Least privilege.] How much of the authority G1 alone would pass downstream does inference remove, per
    component ($C$, $N$, $\plain$, $D$), at what missing authority, and how often is a proposal rejected at $\ceil$?
  \item[RQ2 Recovery.] Does bounding recovery by $A$ keep injected instructions from reaching authority outside the
    task, compared with a parent-bounded rule, and what does it cost legitimate tasks?
  \item[RQ3 Robustness.] How much of the RQ1 benefit survives planted injections, injection styles never seen in
    training, an adaptive attacker, and an inflated manifest?
  \item[RQ4 Cost.] What latency does inference add per delegation, and how often does the helper refuse?
  \item[RQ5 Training.] Does task-specific training let small, fast helpers match the best prompted helper on RQ1--RQ3,
    including on task families and attack styles never seen in training?
\end{description}

\section{Datasets}\label{sec:data}
\paragraph{Policy model of every task.} Each task is one delegation: task text, receiver (identity, local or remote,
$\ceil$), delegator holdings $(C_i, N_i, \plain_i, D_i)$, upstream context, optional manifest, and an optional injected
instruction with the authority it targets. Labels give, per component, the minimal authority (required) and what an
alternative valid plan adds (plausible). Following \BC{}'s review, permissions are restricted to what \BC{} enforces:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{$C$:} S3 atoms (s3, $o$, bucket, key prefix), $o\in\{$GET, PUT, DELETE, LIST$\}$; operations outside these
    verbs are removed. Other services as decided in \S\ref{sec:decisions}.
  \item \emph{$N$ and $\plain$:} a destination is a candidate only if it has no parser, and choosing it places it in
    $\plain'$. Destinations with a parser, peers and the model endpoint are not candidates.
  \item \emph{$D$:} whole volumes $(v,\mu)$, $\mu\in\{\mathrm{ro},\mathrm{rw}\}$, for local receivers only; remote receivers
    have $D'=\emptyset$. No executable permissions.
  \item \emph{Recovery and ceilings:} a task needing a volume it lacks fails; recovery requests name one atom or
    destination; a proposal exceeding $\ceil$ is a rejected delegation.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Labeled evaluation set (RQ1--RQ4).} 408 tasks from 17 templates over the paper's workflows: 288 test and
120 development, with no template in both. Each template varies over 4 name bindings, 3 levels of delegator
over-provisioning (same resource with another verb, sibling resources, wider prefixes), and with or without one
injected instruction aimed at authority the delegator holds but the task does not need. Two reviewers label every
candidate independently and adjudicate; we report Cohen's $\kappa$~\cite{cohen}. Test files are frozen by checksum
before any helper runs; thresholds are tuned on development only.

\paragraph{Synthetic set (training and held-out generalization; RQ5).} No public dataset has this setting, so we
generate one with methods from recent least-privilege datasets~\cite{taskcond,toolpriv,authbench}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Size and split.} 2,000 tasks in 40 families: 1,500 train and 300 validation from 32 families; 200 test tasks
    from 8 families never trained on. Size follows the closest prior work~\cite{taskcond}. No name is shared with the
    labeled set, and tasks sharing any 8-word sequence with it are dropped~\cite{contamination}.
  \item \emph{Generation.} A local open model writes the task text as a delegator would, plus the API calls that complete
    it and optionally an alternative valid plan~\cite{selfinstruct,apigen}. Receivers vary over local and remote.
  \item \emph{Labels from execution.} Each call runs against a mock service that logs the atoms, destinations and volumes it
    uses. The required tier is the minimal candidate set covering the log, as IAM Access Analyzer derives policies from
    activity~\cite{accessanalyzer}; the plausible tier is what the alternative plan adds. Each call's permission comes from
    the provider's published reference (for S3, AWS's machine-readable service reference). The labeler reproduces the
    human labels on all 408 labeled tasks.
  \item \emph{Checks.} Format, mock execution, and two judge models other than the writer that must both accept the
    task~\cite{toolpriv,apigen}; near-duplicates removed; yield reported per stage.
  \item \emph{Injections.} Half the tasks carry one; its label does not change.
  \item \emph{Human acceptance test.} Two reviewers label 93 random tasks blind and adjudicate. The set is accepted if at
    most one generated label is wrong, showing a label error rate below 5\% with 95\% confidence~\cite{montgomery,clopper}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{External benchmarks.} FORTIS tasks 1 and 2~\cite{fortis} and the mostargate prompts~\cite{noyan}. Evaluation only.

\paragraph{End-to-end runs in \BC{} (RQ1, RQ2, RQ4).} The paper's object-storage workflows in \BC{}'s harness
(S3-compatible store, stub internet, scripted and live agents, deterministic oracle with canary strings), with the
subprocess proposer running each final helper through the adapter. We measure denials, recovery requests, ceiling
rejections, task completion, oracle-detected attack outcomes, and delegation latency through the tunnel.

\paragraph{Attack suite (RQ3).} On the labeled test set and the end-to-end runs: (a) the planted injections; (b) injection
styles from published corpora never used in training~\cite{injecagent,agentdojo}; (c) an adaptive attacker that knows the
design, candidate format and output schema, has the open weights, and searches for text that places a target candidate
in the proposal or in $A$ under a fixed query budget~\cite{carlini,attackersecond,pair}, reported as success versus
budget; (d) a receiver operator who signs a manifest listing the attack's target as usually needed.

\section{Training}\label{sec:training}
Training cannot add a guarantee; G1 supplies those. It changes precision and cost: the fast helpers failed on task
knowledge, not on the mechanism (Table~\ref{tab:prelim}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Targets.} Generative helpers learn the three-tier index output; per-candidate scorers learn graded targets
    (1, 0.5, 0) so their scores separate the tiers~\cite{beyondyesno}; CAPMAS uses its own contrastive training code.
  \item \emph{Method.} Full fine-tuning up to 8B~\cite{loralearnsless}; Qwen3-30B-A3B uses QLoRA~\cite{qlora}, compared
    only at matching precision.
  \item \emph{Settings.} Cosine schedule, 3\% warm-up, 2 epochs; learning rate from $\{5\!\times\!10^{-6}, 10^{-5},
    2\!\times\!10^{-5}\}$ on synthetic validation; three seeds.
  \item \emph{Isolation.} No labeled, external, end-to-end or attack data is used for training or selection.
\end{itemize}

\section{Metrics and statistics}\label{sec:stats}
Per task and per component $X\in\{C,N,\plain,D\}$, against the minimal authority $X^\star$, on witness atoms so a prefix
counts for everything it covers:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Excess authority} $|\den{X'}\setminus\den{X^\star}| / |\den{X_i}\setminus\den{X^\star}|$ (set-only $=1$);
    \textbf{missing authority} $|\den{X^\star}\setminus\den{X'}| / |\den{X^\star}|$; exact match.
  \item \textbf{Ceiling rejections}: share of proposals rejected at $\ceil$.
  \item \textbf{Attack reachability after recovery}: share of attacks whose target is granted at delegation or reachable
    within $k=3$ requests (sensitivity $k\in\{1,5\}$), under $A$-bounded and parent-bounded admission.
  \item \textbf{Task completion} (a missing volume, a refusal or a ceiling rejection is a failure); recovery requests per
    task; refusal rate.
  \item \textbf{Benefit retained under attack} $(E_{\mathrm{G1}}-E_{\mathrm{attack}})/(E_{\mathrm{G1}}-E_{\mathrm{clean}})$.
  \item \textbf{Latency}: helper time per call (median, p95), and delegation setup time end to end.
\end{itemize}
Helpers, metrics, comparisons and pass rules are fixed in our decision log before results are seen. Primary comparisons,
each Holm-corrected within its metric: best helper vs.\ set-only (RQ1); $A$-bounded vs.\ parent-bounded recovery (RQ2);
each fine-tuned helper vs.\ its untrained version and vs.\ prompted Qwen3-8B (RQ5); best helper vs.\ CAPMAS and vs.\ each
coin flip. 95\% intervals by bootstrap over templates or task families; McNemar's exact test for paired binary outcomes.

\section{Open decisions and the \BC{} asks}\label{sec:decisions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Services beyond S3.} Only S3 and httpapi have parsers. We propose keeping the issue-tracker, document and
    chat workflows as httpapi atoms if httpapi's atom form fits them; otherwise their destinations are $\plain$ only and
    capability results come from S3 alone.
  \item Local or remote receiver for each handoff, and $\ceil$ for each receiver role.
  \item The cap $k$ (we propose 3) and the helper timeout (prototype: 60\,s).
  \item Which destinations are $\plain$ in each workflow.
  \item Whether end-to-end runs may log helper candidates, tiers and $A$.
\end{enumerate}
